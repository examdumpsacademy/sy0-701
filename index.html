CompTIA Security+ SY0-701: Exam Guide, Objectives and Study Plan

If you are looking at entry-level cybersecurity roles, you will see the same line in job posting after job posting: "Security+ preferred" or "Security+ required." The SY0-701 exam is the version of that certification that most candidates are sitting right now, and it is a tougher, more practical test than the older versions many study guides still describe.

This guide covers what the exam actually looks like, what each domain expects from you, how long preparation realistically takes, and the mistakes that cost people points. It also covers something a lot of articles skip: SY0-701 has a published retirement date, and that should shape when you book your test.

Timing matters. CompTIA has listed June 11, 2027 as the retirement date for the English SY0-701 exam, with its successor, SY0-801, expected to launch on or around November 17, 2026. Details are in the section on SY0-701 versus SY0-801.

What Is the CompTIA Security+ SY0-701 Certification?

Security+ is a vendor-neutral certification from CompTIA that validates the baseline skills needed to work in a security role. "Vendor-neutral" means the exam is not tied to Microsoft, Cisco, AWS, or any single product. You are tested on concepts and decision-making, not on where to click in one vendor's console.

SY0-701 is the current exam code. It launched in November 2023 and replaced SY0-601. The change was more than cosmetic. CompTIA reduced the number of objectives, shifted attention toward security operations, and added more material on zero trust, automation, and governance. The people the exam targets are security analysts, junior security engineers, and system or network administrators who handle security tasks.

Who Should Take the SY0-701 Exam?

Security+ fits a fairly wide range of people, but it pays off most for these groups:

  • Help desk and IT support staff who want to move into security, SOC, or systems roles.
  • Network and system administrators whose duties already include firewalls, access control, patching, and logging.
  • Career changers with some IT exposure who need a recognized credential to get interviews.
  • Government and defense contractor staff who need a certification that meets role-based requirements. Security+ is widely used for this under U.S. Department of Defense workforce rules, but the exact requirement depends on your position, so confirm it with your employer or contracting officer.

CompTIA recommends Network+ and about two years of experience in a security or systems administration role. Those are recommendations, not prerequisites. Plenty of people pass without either, but they usually spend more time on networking basics, because a surprising number of Security+ questions assume you already understand ports, subnets, and how traffic moves.

How the scoring works

The 100 to 900 scale is not a simple percentage. CompTIA does not publish how many questions you must get right, and PBQs are generally believed to carry more weight than a standard multiple-choice item, though the company does not spell out the formula. The practical takeaway: do not assume 750 means "83 percent." Aim for consistent practice scores well above the line.

What performance-based questions feel like

PBQs are small simulations. You might be asked to drag the correct controls onto a network diagram, place firewall rules in the right order, or match attack types to descriptions. They appear at the start of the exam, and they eat time. A sensible habit is to flag them, answer the multiple-choice questions first, then come back with the clock in mind. You will not be penalized for the order in which you work.

Skills Measured: The Five SY0-701 Domains

DomainWeight
1.0 General Security Concepts12%
2.0 Threats, Vulnerabilities, and Mitigations22%
3.0 Security Architecture18%
4.0 Security Operations28%
5.0 Security Program Management and Oversight20%

Weights are approximate and set by CompTIA. Download the official exam objectives PDF from CompTIA and treat it as your checklist; every other resource is a means of covering it.

Domain 1: General Security Concepts (12%)

This is the foundation. You will need to tell apart control categories (technical, managerial, operational, physical) and control types (preventive, deterrent, detective, corrective, compensating, directive). Expect the CIA triad, non-repudiation, and the AAA model. Zero trust shows up here, as do change management basics and the cryptography toolkit: symmetric and asymmetric encryption, hashing, salting, digital signatures, certificates, and PKI.

A common trap is mixing up control category and control type. A security camera is a physical control by category and can be detective or deterrent by type. Practice sorting examples both ways.

Domain 2: Threats, Vulnerabilities, and Mitigations (22%)

Here you learn who attacks, why, and how. The domain covers threat actors and their motivations, attack surfaces, social engineering techniques such as phishing and pretexting, malware families, application attacks like injection and cross-site scripting, and network attacks such as denial of service and on-path attacks. It also covers mitigation: segmentation, patching, least privilege, hardening, and monitoring.

The exam likes scenarios. Instead of asking you to define ransomware, it describes symptoms on a file server and asks for the best next step. Learn each threat well enough to recognize it from its behavior, not just its name.

Domain 3: Security Architecture (18%)

This domain asks how secure environments are designed. Topics include cloud models and shared responsibility, on-premises infrastructure, infrastructure as code, containers and virtualization, IoT and industrial control systems, network appliances, secure communication, data classification and protection, and resilience through backups, high availability, and site strategies.

If you have never touched a cloud platform, spend an evening in a free tier of one. Reading about security groups is not the same as creating one and watching traffic get blocked.

Domain 4: Security Operations (28%)

This is the heaviest domain and the one that reflects the daily job. It includes secure baselines and hardening, asset management, vulnerability scanning and analysis, alerting and monitoring, identity and access management (including SSO, MFA, and federation), automation, and incident response. It also covers the data sources you would use during an investigation, such as logs, packet captures, and SIEM output.

Give this domain more study hours than its percentage suggests. The incident response process and log interpretation questions are where unprepared candidates lose the most ground.

Domain 5: Security Program Management and Oversight (20%)

Many technical candidates underestimate this one. It covers governance, policies and standards, risk assessment and treatment, third-party and vendor risk, compliance, audits, privacy, and security awareness training. You will see terms like risk appetite, risk register, SLE, ALE, and RTO/RPO, and you need to be comfortable with the simple calculations behind some of them.

How to Prepare for SY0-701: A Realistic Study Plan

How long you need depends on your starting point. As rough guidance, someone with a year or two of IT experience can often prepare in 8 to 12 weeks studying a few hours a week. Someone new to IT may need closer to four or five months, especially if networking is unfamiliar. These are estimates, not promises, and your own practice scores are a better guide than any calendar.

Step 1: Read the official objectives first

Spend an hour with the objectives document. Highlight every term you cannot explain in a sentence. That list becomes your study map.

Step 2: Pick one primary learning source

Choose either a video course, a textbook, or CompTIA's own learning platform, and stick with it. Candidates who jump between three courses tend to re-learn the same easy topics and neglect the hard ones.

Step 3: Study by domain, then test each domain

After finishing a domain, take a short quiz on it before moving on. Retrieval practice beats rereading, and it exposes weak spots while there is still time to fix them.

Step 4: Add hands-on practice

You do not need an expensive lab. Install Wireshark and capture your own traffic. Run a vulnerability scanner against a home lab or intentionally vulnerable VM. Set up a free SIEM or log viewer. Even a few hours of this makes PBQs feel much less foreign.

Step 5: Take full-length timed practice exams

In the last two to three weeks, sit full practice tests under exam conditions: 90 minutes, no notes, no pausing. Review every wrong answer and every right answer you guessed. When you are scoring comfortably above 80 percent on quality practice material across several different tests, you are likely close to ready. Practice scores are an indicator, not a guarantee.

Step 6: Plan the final week

Skip learning new material in the last two days. Review your notes on port numbers, acronyms, and the incident response lifecycle, sleep properly, and confirm your testing appointment details.

Common Challenges and How to Handle Them

Acronym overload

Security+ is full of them: SIEM, SOAR, DLP, EDR, CASB, SASE, RTO, RPO, MTTR. Build flashcards and say each one out loud with a one-line meaning and a real example. Memorizing the letters without the concept does not help on scenario questions.

"Choose the BEST answer" questions

Often two or three options are technically reasonable. The exam wants the one that most directly solves the stated problem with the least disruption or cost. Read the last sentence of the question first, then the scenario, so you know what you are solving for.

Time pressure on PBQs

Flag them, finish the multiple-choice section, then return. Leave enough time to check your drag-and-drop work, because it is easy to misplace one item.

Weak networking background

If subnetting, DNS, DHCP, and common ports feel shaky, fix that early. The security concepts are much easier to absorb when the underlying network makes sense.

Studying only practice questions

Memorizing question banks can create a false sense of readiness. The real exam rewrites scenarios, so understanding why an answer is right matters more than recognizing a familiar wording.

Recommended Resources

  • CompTIA's official SY0-701 exam objectives: free, and the single most important document. Everything on the test traces back to it.
  • CompTIA's official study materials: the official study guide, plus the CertMaster learning and lab products, if you prefer content written directly against the objectives.
  • Video courses from established instructors: useful for visual learners and for topics like cryptography, where seeing a diagram helps.
  • Reputable practice exam providers: look for explanations on every answer, not just a score. Be wary of any site that promises "real exam questions"; CompTIA prohibits sharing live exam content, and "brain dump" material can get your certification revoked.
  • Hands-on tools: Wireshark, Nmap, a free-tier cloud account, and a virtual machine platform for experimenting safely.
  • Community forums: candidates who recently passed often share their experience with PBQs and pacing. Treat individual anecdotes as color, not as official guidance.

SY0-701 vs SY0-801: Which Exam Should You Take?

CompTIA has announced the next version, SY0-801 (often called Security+ V8). According to CompTIA's certification pages, it is expected to launch on or around November 17, 2026, and the objectives are published. The company has also listed June 11, 2027 as the retirement date for the English SY0-701 exam, and August 13, 2027 for the Japanese, Portuguese, Spanish, and Thai versions. Reports covering the new objectives indicate that V8 adds AI-related security content and shifts further toward security operations.

For you, the decision mostly comes down to your realistic test date:

  • Ready to test before mid-November 2026: SY0-701 is your only option, and the study material is mature.
  • Testing between the SY0-801 launch and June 11, 2027: you can choose either. If you are already well into SY0-701 prep, finishing it is perfectly reasonable.
  • Testing after June 11, 2027: you will need SY0-801 for the English exam.

Three practical points. First, the certification itself says "CompTIA Security+" regardless of version, and it stays valid for three years from the date you pass. Second, check the exam code on any voucher you buy, because a voucher does not extend a version's retirement date. Third, launch dates can shift, so confirm everything on CompTIA's website before you commit money or a testing appointment.

Career Benefits of Earning Security+

Security+ will not turn you into a senior engineer, and it is honest to say so. What it does is help you clear the first filter. Many employers and government contractors use it as a baseline requirement, and it signals that you understand core security vocabulary and can reason through common scenarios.

Common roles where the certification is relevant include:

  • Security analyst or SOC analyst (entry level)
  • Systems or network administrator with security duties
  • Junior security engineer
  • Help desk or IT support moving toward security
  • Compliance or risk support roles

Salaries vary widely by country, city, clearance level, and prior experience, so be skeptical of any single figure quoted without a source. Look at current job postings in your own market to see what employers are actually offering.

Security+ is also a sensible stepping stone. Many people follow it with a more specialized credential such as CompTIA CySA+ or PenTest+, or a broader one like (ISC)² CISSP once they have the required experience. Because CompTIA allows you to renew Security+ by earning continuing education units or by passing a higher-level CompTIA exam, those next steps can keep your credential current at the same time.

Frequently Asked Questions About SY0-701

What is the passing score for SY0-701?

You need 750 on a scale of 100 to 900. CompTIA does not publish the exact number of questions required, so it is better to aim for strong, consistent practice scores than to count questions.

How many questions are on the SY0-701 exam?

Up to 90 questions, which include both multiple-choice questions and performance-based questions, in 90 minutes.

Is SY0-701 hard?

It is demanding for beginners because the scope is broad, but it is considered an entry-level to intermediate exam. The difficulty is less about depth and more about breadth and scenario-based wording. Candidates with prior IT experience generally find it more manageable.

How long should I study for SY0-701?

It varies. Many candidates with some IT background study for roughly two to three months; those new to the field may need longer. Track your readiness through domain quizzes and full practice exams rather than a fixed schedule.

Do I need Network+ before Security+?

No. CompTIA recommends it, but it is not required. If you skip it, make sure you understand networking fundamentals before starting security topics.

How long is Security+ valid?

Three years from the date you pass. You can renew by earning continuing education units through CompTIA's program or by passing a higher-level CompTIA certification.

Can I still take SY0-701 now?

Yes. As of this writing, SY0-701 is available, and CompTIA has listed June 11, 2027 as the retirement date for the English version. Confirm the current status on CompTIA's site before booking.

What happens if I fail?

You can retake the exam, but each attempt requires a new fee unless you bought a bundle that includes a retake. CompTIA sets waiting periods between attempts, so check the current retake policy on their website. If you fail, use the score report to see which domains were weakest and rebuild your plan around them.

Does Security+ meet DoD requirements?

It is widely used to satisfy U.S. Department of Defense baseline certification requirements, but the specific requirements depend on the role and the current policy. Always confirm with your employer or program office.

Conclusion

SY0-701 rewards candidates who understand how security works in practice: reading a scenario, spotting the risk, and choosing the most sensible control. If you map your study to the official objectives, give extra time to Security Operations, practice with hands-on tools, and test yourself under timed conditions, you will walk into the exam knowing what to expect.

Check CompTIA's site for the current price, policies, and version dates, decide on SY0-701 or SY0-801 based on when you can realistically test, and book the exam date early. Having a date on the calendar does more for consistent studying than almost any other tactic.
